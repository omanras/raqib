## 3. Findings: silent assumptions and what a rule should have caught (A3, A5)

### Your item 1: L-1

What I checked and found sound (all certain from the code):
- passed() is reached only through took, settle, a skip, or a local refusal. settle (771-781) runs only on a "taken"
  answer, for pending entries that are contiguous and before the taken group.
- pending is emptied after settle. No line is counted twice, and none is sent after it is counted (the cursor is past
  it).
- alone lines are popped in order. queue lines are consumed in order, and more(reach) continues from reach.
- A failure, a token answer or Stop inside resolve returns with the cursor before the first line in question.
- A kill between settle and write_state leaves the older state, so lines are sent again, not counted twice.
- A restart re-sends the lines in question from the cursor (Q3).
- A collector that takes the batch but refuses lines alone never sees them alone.
- A collector that refuses the batch and takes each line alone gets one request and one fsync'd state write per line.
  That is slow, but it does not loop and does not duplicate.
- E1's [a, b, c] sequence is exactly 400, 200, 400, 200 (test 874-888).

8. Q1, the cap (forward.py:64, 938, 984-989). After 100 lines in question the try ends; line 101 is never sent.
   - Every later try sends the same batch and the same 100 lines alone again.
      - So a run of at least 100 consecutive lines that a collector refuses alone stalls the forwarder for as long as
     it runs, even where that collector would take line 101.
      - The state shows failing `refusing` with the collector's status and rising tries. The cursor and `sent` are
     frozen and `behind` grows.
      - `--status` gives 1 FAILING, the same as for a collector that takes nothing. The count in question is only in
     the output (Q3).
   - Nothing passes the lines short of editing the state, or moving it away (and `--from end` then skips everything).
   - test_forward.py:959-964 pins this behaviour. Nothing unproven is passed and nothing is lost.
   - Certain. Likely rare.
9. Refusals that are not the line's, on a collector that takes some lines (resolve 970-975 then settle 771-781).
   - Example: a load-balanced collector with one node answering 404, or a transient 400.
      - The lines refused alone are counted and passed once a neighbour is taken. They are lost, and `--status` is 0
     with the refused count rising.
   - This is what L-1's words permit. It narrows J-4's hole without closing it. Suspicion against A3.
   - Certain from the code; plausible behind a load balancer.
10. Q2 (resolve 949-959). Non-event and over-long lines after a line in question do not count toward the cap.
        - One try then walks every such line to the end of the file. It keeps one pending tuple per line, with no
      stopped() check and no state write in that loop.

    - Certain; low.
11. File identity inside a try.
    - more() (854-860) reopens the file by path with no same_file check.
        - If the file is replaced during a try that has lines in question, a take in the new file moves the cursor to

    - Certain; low.
12. Arrival order at the collector is not promised away, and the documents do not say so.
        - (a) Certain from the code: c is sent alone while b is in question, and c's answer is lost (`late`) after the

### Your item 2: L-4

What I checked and found sound:
- One Send thread per try. The main thread waits in 0.25 s slices on time.monotonic against a deadline per phase:
  connect plus handshake 10 s from the try's start, name resolution included (370, 376-377, 386-387); sending 30 s
  from its first byte; the answer 30 s from the request's last byte.
- Abandon means shutdown, then join(0.5), then detach and close, then join(0.5). Stop is honoured within about 1.25 s
  plus one state write.
- The abandoned thread touches only its own Send object. The token is read in send() before the Send is built. The
  cursor, the state and fwd._send are not touched, and the shared SSLContext is only read.
- `self.mono` is `mono` (514). pause, the beat (1051) and written_at (571) use it; sliced_wait and Send use
  time.monotonic. The wall clock is used only by iso().
- Certain.

14. Q9. A thread blocked in getaddrinfo outlives its abandonment, holding its Send: the body up to 512 KiB, and
  headers with the token.
        - With a resolver that never returns, there is one per try: about one every 70 s, about 1200 a day, with
      memory unbounded over weeks.
    - The main loop goes on, so `--status` says FAILING `connect` (an alarm), not SILENT.
    - FORWARD.md:100-103 does not say that threads accumulate.
    - Certain from the code. Whether a real resolver can block that long I could not check.
        - Sockets do not pile up: a thread blocked on a socket ends at shutdown, or at its own per-operation timeout
      of at most 30 s.
15. The close fallback (474-481) closes an fd number while the worker may still be inside an SSL call.
    - OpenSSL's BIO holds the fd number, and SSLSocket has no detach of its own.
        - If the number is reused meanwhile (the next connection, the state temp file), the abandoned thread could
      read or write it.
        - It is reached only where shutdown did not end the thread within 0.5 s. On Linux, shutdown wakes poll.
      Windows I could not check.
    - Low; reasoned, not observed.
