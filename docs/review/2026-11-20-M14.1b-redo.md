**1. A restart inside a round is not bounded by the round's time (BW-4, E4, BU-3).**

Where: `ProcEvents._ended` (host.py:697) and the REFUSED path (host.py:750) call `_retire()`, which calls
  `helper.stop()` (host.py:691). `stop()` then waits:
- `proc.wait(STOP_SECONDS)`, 2 s (procevents.py:1151);
- after `terminate`, up to 1 s (procevents.py:1155-1156);
- after `kill`, up to 1 s (procevents.py:1159-1160);
- `join(KILL_SECONDS)`, 1 s (procevents.py:1165).

All of this runs inside `round()`.

Smallest code change:
- procevents.py:1138 becomes `def stop(self, seconds=STOP_SECONDS):`.
- In `stop()`, use `seconds` in place of STOP_SECONDS at 1151 and 1153.
- Use `min(KILL_SECONDS, seconds)` at 1156, 1160 and 1165.
- host.py:679 becomes `def _retire(self, seconds=None):`.
- host.py:691 calls `helper.stop() if seconds is None else helper.stop(seconds)`.
- host.py:697 and host.py:750 pass `self.timeout`. `close()` keeps the default.
- tests/test_host_procevents.py:80: `StandIn.stop` takes `seconds=None`.
- In tests/test_host_procevents.py:179-188, time each `events.round()` and assert the slowest is under 0.5 s. Today
  rounds 3 and 6 take about 1 s, because the pump's `join(1.0)` waits on the fake's still-open pipe.

If the Table defers the code to M14.1c, the minimum instead is:

## FINDINGS

**M1 (medium; a changed result, not a hang): a restart round can be late at short intervals.**
- What is wrong: the restart's stop runs inside the round (Must fix 1 has the lines).
- A SIGSTOPped real helper does not end when its input closes, and SIGTERM stays pending on a stopped task until
  SIGCONT. Only the kill ends it. That is about 2 + 1 s inside the third late round.
- A merely slow helper at `--every 0.1` (25 ms asks, about 225 ms without any answer) gets the same restart. Its exit
  (Reader stop, poll 0.25 s) takes about 0.3 to 2 s, against a 0.2 s deadline.
- The late round is a `reader-late` hole of the result (host.py:2485-2492), which BU-3 forbids and E4 denies.
- At the default T of 10 s, or the rig's 5 s, nothing changes.
- Also: `Helper.start()` calls `stop()` (2 s) when its thread cannot be made (procevents.py:1049). That is rare.

**M2 (low; a hang reachable only by a writer that is not the real helper).**
- What is wrong: the ask is a blocking write and flush (procevents.py:1110-1112), and ask numbers are sequential
  (1109).
- Suppose something writes correctly numbered answers into the helper's output while the helper does not read its
  input (the helper stopped, or a "wrong" helper). Then each round writes one more ask. After about 64 KiB of asks the
  write blocks while `ProcEvents.lock` is held, and Stop's `close()` waits on that lock for ever (host.py:772, 4912).
- The real `serve` answers only lines it has read, so it cannot do this.
- A process of the same user can write into that user's processes' pipes. It can also stop the user watch itself, so
  this is no new power over the user watch, and it does not reach the unit's watch.
- THREAT_MODEL:535-536 "wrong ... never its Stop" claims more (Must fix 3).
- This is the L1 hang path, still open in this form.

**M3 (low-medium; a crash of the report caused by a line's content).**
- What is wrong: `_pe_num` (host.py:594-595) accepts any non-negative int. `json.loads` accepts integers of up to 4300
  digits.
- The watch then adds them up across helpers and rounds: `_lost` (671), `beyond` (676) and `s["unread_exe"]`
  (3220-3221, which grows each round where `missed` goes down and back up).
- Converting a sum of more than 4300 digits to text raises ValueError (Python 3.11+ limit): `"%d"` at 3274, 3296,
  3297, `%s` at 3289, and `json.dumps` of the inventory.
- That ValueError reaches `tick()` and `loop()`, which report CRASHED (host.py:5701-5704), and probably the final
  report too.
- It goes against BW-8's "whatever a helper's line holds ... goes on". Only a forged or modified writer can cause it.
- How I know: by reading. I did not run it.

**M4 (low; false statements).**
- PROC-EVENTS.md:287-289 "the next round asks again" is false under BW-1 after both `late` and `not a line`. A `not a
  line` at the client level (procevents.py:1121-1123) keeps `outstanding`, so a single bad line costs three late
  rounds and the one restart (host.py:754-757).
- THREAT_MODEL.md:533-536 is covered in Must fix 3.
- BETWEEN_HEARD (host.py:398-401) says the helper read the events "for the whole watch". By the builder's choice 3,
  the `starting` rounds (host.py:745-746, 3219) do not count against the healthy words. Nothing is heard before the
  helper's Reader listens: the Python start-up, up to 5 s by design.
- These are small and their gaps are said in the section ("before the helper was ready: N"). For the Table.

**M5 (low-medium; silent assumption, A3).**
- PROC-EVENTS.md:249-257 and THREAT_MODEL.md:523-527 say the helper "loads Raqib from where the watch did and from
  nowhere else". That is true of Raqib.
- But before the fixed program replaces `sys.path`, the interpreter's start-up runs site's work:
  - under `-I` and `-P`: site-packages `.pth` files (their `import` lines run) and `sitecustomize`;
  - under `-P` only: also PYTHONPATH, the user site and `usercustomize`.
- These are the places the watch trusted at its own start, but they are read again at each start and restart. A
  user-site folder or `.pth` file made after the watch started runs in a process the watch records as its own (BH-3).
- The watch's `-E`, `-s` and `-S` are not carried: only `-I` is copied (procevents.py:960-962).
- The questions file's choice 8 (lines 32-36) names only PYTHONPATH for `import json`.
- So BW-5's FOR, "trusts nothing the watch did not already trust at its own start", holds for the places, not for
  their contents at the time of the restart. The documents do not say this.

**M6 (low; tests that would not fail if the behaviour were removed).**
- The `STARTING` branch of `ProcEvents.round` (host.py:745-746): no host test drives `starting` through `round()`.
  `fake_client` says READY at popen, and `test_e4_a_round_before...` feeds a dict.
- The "no ask was answered" fallback (host.py:3277-3278): without it, a watch whose every round was `starting` prints
  the healthy words. Nothing tests it.
- `-P` and `cwd="/"`: only the argv and kw echo tests pin them (test_procevents_helper.py:149-159, 245). The real E5
  test (451-464) passes with either one removed.
- Must fix 4 covers the E4 lateness test and the 2 MiB test.

**M7 (low; tests that depend on real time).**
- test_host_procevents.py:309 asserts each 25 ms ask took under 75 ms. That could flake on a loaded runner.
- The 10000-round tests bound only the total time: under 20 s at test_procevents_helper.py:298, under 30 s at
  test_host_procevents.py:185. Both are generous.
- In the host test, 9994 of the 10000 rounds run against a final helper, so it proves little beyond rounds 1-6. It
  does catch a second write through `close() == []`.
- The retired fakes' pump threads stay blocked for the life of the test process, because their pipes' write ends are
  never closed. This is harmless.

**M8 (low; a statement that may be false).**
- On a late-three-times restart, an answer still unread is dropped with its numbers, along with the old helper's
  counts after its last answer (host.py:679-688). "lost: none" (host.py:3288) can then stand for "not known after
  round N".
- The restart line and the withheld healthy words say the gap.
- Also: when the second start fails, the section prints "started again, round N: ended for good ... and this start"
  (host.py:701-703, 654-656, 3301-3302) for a start that did not happen.

**M9 (low; checks the client does not make).**
- `"v": 1.0` is accepted (procevents.py:1071).
- The client does not enforce SERVE_MAX. A 1 MiB line of about 100k tiny records is checked and counted after the
  deadline (host.py:705-732).
- `counted` is bounded by key count, not by bytes: a long `began` text is kept as part of a key (host.py:720-725).
- All three need a forged writer.

**M10 (low; X35).**
- X35 is MET where `past the reader's bounds` > 0 or the last `more` was true (the builder's choice 10, outside BW-7's
  list). The report then withholds the healthy words while the rig says MET.
- The refusal is checked first (check.py:878-882). A refusal naming EPERM or EACCES at the second start therefore
  reads n/a, which hides the restart and any loss.
- The `began` floor cannot fail on the rig. BW-7 accepts this as a floor, and it is said in the row's words.

**BU-4 (none found).**
- No program path, `unread` text or record field reaches any output. Only numbers do, plus three of the helper's
  texts, each passed through `_pe_words` (host.py:570-573: printable ASCII, one line, at most 300 characters):
  - `refused`: shown in the section, the hole, the terminal, the blind list, the notes and the state;
  - `lost.why`: one per helper, at most two helpers;
  - `counts.ended`: shown in the hole.
- A forged writer can place up to 300 printable characters of its choice in each. That is bounded and single-line, and
  acceptable while nothing is judged. M14.1c must not treat these lines as authenticated.
- I found no path where an exception message carrying helper text reaches `problems` (host.py:744).

## FOR M14.1c
- The helper's lines are not authenticated. A process of the same user can write into that user's processes' pipes, so
  names and counts read from them can be chosen by an agent. Every name must go through BU-4's name gate, and the
  healthy words can be forged by a process that stops the helper and answers in its place.
- If Must fix 1 is deferred: bound the old helper's stop by the round.
- Make the ask's write non-blocking: a full pipe should be an end, not a block (M2).
- Decide whether the helper should always run `-I`. The path is given anyway, so `-E`, `-s` and `-P` lose nothing.
  Also say what runs before the fixed program (M5).
- Decide whether a child under the helper is a finding (BH-3, L5, BW-5).
- Settle the "(deleted)" image (BW-9) and holes of the result for refused, late or ended helpers (BW-9, Q1).
- Decide whether `starting` rounds should withhold the healthy words or the words should say "from the helper's first
  line" (M4).
- A single client-level `not a line` costs the one restart through three late rounds. Decide whether that is meant.
- Bound the entries per answer on the client side (SERVE_MAX) and the bytes kept in `counted` (M9).
- Decide whether X35 should name `beyond` and `more`, and check restarts before an n/a refusal (M10).
- A loss after a retired helper's last answer is unknown; say it as such (M8).
