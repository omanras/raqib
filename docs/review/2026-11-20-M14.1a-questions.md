## The REDO (Amendment BR, review 2026-11-20-M14.1a.md)

Choices where the REDO's HANDOFF was silent:

1. **Threads (BR-1).** The threads of a record are the pids of the forks
   whose child tgid is its pid, less those whose exit was heard; at most
   PROCESSES_MAX of them per process (past it: `beyond`). An exec clears them
   (an exec leaves one thread). The measured order (a) needs nothing more:
   the leader's exit comes while the thread is known, the exec follows, the
   real exit ends the process. The other order (the exec before the
   leader's exit), not measured, would end the process at the leader's exit.
2. **The same process after an exit.** An ended record is remembered by its
   pid (at most PROCESSES_MAX, the oldest forgotten) until a fork of that
   pid; an exec of the pid takes it up again where the start read is its own
   (or either is not known). Its end, given before, is then not its end: the
   record is given again with `ended` none. Its threads were not known when
   its leader ended (a lost fork, or a process older than the reader).
3. **Unread keys.** A failed read of /proc/PID/stat is under `stat` (it
   gives the parent and the start), the link under `exe`; a fork-only
   record's are `exe` and `stat`. `threads` is a key of `unread` where the
   count of threads is not known (`lost` not 0; a process older than the
   reader).
4. **Earlier programs (BR-2).** `earlier` holds `{exe, unread}` for each
   exec before the last; the time before the first exec (the parent's
   program, not read) is not an entry.
5. **The holder (BR-3).** The exec's kernel time (the monotonic clock) is
   put on the boot clock with the difference of the two clocks at the
   datagram's receipt, since stat's start is on the boot clock (time asleep
   would otherwise make every read look late). The ticks of a second are the
   system's (`SC_CLK_TCK`), a test's 100. A later exec whose start differs
   from the record's own is counted `reused` too. Seen here: 3 rig runs and
   the real test, `reused` 0 every time.
6. **The mark (BR-5).** 4 bytes of `os.urandom`, little-endian, taken at the
   start; `urandom` is an argument.
7. **`running`.** A second `start()` of a running reader returns the word
   `running` (the constant RUNNING); a start of a stopped or refused reader
   is a refusal (it begins with the reader's prefix).
8. **The receive queue (R3).** The size is read back (Linux gives it
   doubled); less than the 1 MiB asked is said in `said`.
9. **`ends` carry `began` (R4)**, so an end is joined to its beginning by
   more than the pid.
10. **X31 (BR-7).** The run records its own pid (`run_pid`); each act's
    `ppid` must be it. A refusal whose words name EPERM or EACCES is n/a;
    any other refusal on Linux is MISSED.
11. **The real test (R7).** It starts `true` five times, needs each heard
    with its code, its parent and its end, and asks for each one's program:
    a program not read must say why (`unread`), and the count and the why
    are a warning of the test, not a failure.

What this sandbox showed after the REDO (Linux 6.18, 2 CPUs, as root; not a
field measure): the real test, three runs, 3 or 4 of 5 programs of `true`
not read in time (ENOENT); the rig, three runs, once MET and twice 3 of 14
programs not read (touch, cp, sh; mv, cp, rm); nothing lost, nothing
reused, every parent the run's own. Five children started back to back miss
more here than one child at a time did before the REDO (0 to 4 of 5 then).

## The first build's choices, as they stand

- The kernel's answer is waited for (2 s); an errno or no answer is a
  refusal.
- The stop sends op 2 (ignore) before it closes the socket.
- `code` and `signal` are read from the wait status as wait(2) does; the
  event's exit_signal (SIGCHLD to the parent) is not kept.
- The parent is the fork's where the fork was heard, else the stat's (the
  parent now).
- A process given before that execs again is given again, whole.
- `began` and `ended` are the kernel's stamps put on the wall clock.
- The bind is (the reader's pid, 1): one Reader in a process.
- The rig's acts are the run's own children against this machine alone
  (loopback, `localhost`); its thread does nothing between two acts.

## For the field, and for the Table

- **The read of the program is a race** the reader's thread runs inside one
  interpreter (BR-9: for M14.1b to measure under the watch). In this
  sandbox it loses often for children of 1 to 6 ms; on the measured images
  it did not lose once in 15 runs.
- **X31** on the three images: now held to the parent as well.
- **Not measured**, as Amendments BQ and BR say: a kernel older than 6.8;
  whether a plain user hears another user's processes; thousands of
  processes a second; a process inside a container; the exec of a thread
  heard before its leader's exit.
