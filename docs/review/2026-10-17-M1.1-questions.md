# M1.1 verdicts and check - design questions

Readings taken by the builder where ADR-0001 sections 6 and 8 or the
handoff left room. Each is the most conservative reading available.

1. **The verdict `invalid`** (listed in ADR section 6 without a
   definition) is given to a policy whose measure, limit, routine period
   or red line is touched by an invalid loader row (`Unknown` with cause
   `invalid`); `unknown` is kept for undeclared shapes, missing rates and
   missing data. Both are red; the split tells the owner whether to fix a
   ledger line or a declaration. Question: is this the intended meaning?

2. **Kill history, not window.** "Stays kill on every later date" needs
   every past snapshot, so the kill rule uses `drawdown_history`: one
   point per snapshot date, each against the peak of the `window_days`
   ending on it (a rolling window). The latest crossing (`drawdown >=
   pct`) is `kill` unless a decision with `closes: <id>` is dated strictly
   after it and on or before as-of; then the verdict is `hold` with the
   closing noted in the reason ("kill of D1 closed by D-x (D2)"). A new
   crossing after the closing decision is `kill` again. A crossing on the
   decision's own date is not closed by it. The one-line measure shown is
   the drawdown at the latest snapshot. Consequence: a snapshot anywhere
   in the history that cannot be valued (no rate) makes the kill
   `unknown`. The `drawdown` measure of M0.3 (window ending at the cut
   date) is unchanged and still serves cap/floor policies that use it.

3. **A stale snapshot does not hide a kill**: an unclosed crossing is
   `kill` even when the latest snapshot is older than allowed; otherwise
   the kill policy is `stale` like any holdings policy.

4. **Routine and red-line matching.** `min_amount` compares the magnitude
   of the row's amount in base currency (`abs(amount) >= min_amount`), as
   announced in M0.2 item 12; a candidate row in a foreign currency with
   no rate is "undecided": with no definite match the verdict is
   `unknown`. `tags` requires every listed tag, `tags_any` at least one.
   A red line looks at every ledger row dated on or before as-of ("ever");
   a matching row is `breach` whatever else is wrong, an invalid row dated
   on or before as-of with no match is `invalid`. A routine with a
   matching row is `hold` even if another row in the period is invalid.

5. **EMPTY-PERIOD** is raised per policy: every ledger-based policy (a
   `sum` measure, or a routine) whose current period (cut at as-of) has
   no ledger rows at all is `missing` with an EMPTY-PERIOD finding citing
   the period; holdings-based policies are not affected. No finding is
   raised for the current month when no policy looks at it. A routine
   checked before its usual day of the month is `missing` (silence is
   red; the `routine` command of M1.2 is the place to look at a period).

6. **The current period of a holdings-based cap or floor** is the month
   of as-of, cut at as-of (shown as "as of <date>"); the month only
   matters for a `months_of` limit, whose trailing window ends at as-of.

7. **Exit 3** covers: policies.yml unreadable, unparseable or holding any
   invalid-class problem (a missing base currency, a duplicate id, an
   undeclared category in a measure); a file-level problem (missing,
   unreadable, bad header, bad YAML, empty) in ledger.csv, holdings.csv
   or decisions.yml; and in rates.csv when it exists. All four files are
   required (ADR section 2 marks only rates.csv optional). Row-level
   problems never stop the run: they are red findings (warn for DUP-ROW)
   and the verdicts they touch are `invalid`.

8. **Green** is every policy `hold` and no red finding; a DUP-ROW warning
   alone keeps the run green (ADR section 3 calls it class warn).

9. **Output.** Policy lines are reds first in file order, then holds in
   file order; each line ends with the scope (`[2026-09]`, `[as of
   2026-09-30]`, `[ever]`) and, for reds, `-- <reason>`; findings that
   belong to no policy are listed under `findings:`; the last line is
   `GREEN|RED as of <date>: ...`. JSON adds `command`, `base_currency`,
   `files` (row counts; `null` when rates.csv is absent) and `problems`.
   Every amount in JSON is decimal text, never a number. Percentages are
   shown with two decimals (display only).

10. **Decisions are loaded by `check`** already (the kill rule needs
    closers); DECISION-DUE itself is M1.2.
