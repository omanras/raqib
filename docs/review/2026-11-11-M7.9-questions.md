## The query (E1), and why it is safe

argv (no shell; Python's list, so `/q:...` is one argument):

    C:\Windows\System32\wevtutil.exe qe Microsoft-Windows-Sysmon/Operational /q:QUERY /c:2000 /f:xml

with the environment `{"SystemRoot": ...}` only and a 10 s timeout (I-5: a helper not back is
terminated, then killed, and recorded as a hole of the round). QUERY is, before any round read a record:

    *[System[(EventID=1 or EventID=3 or EventID=11 or EventID=22) and TimeCreated[@SystemTime>='2026-11-10T07:45:00.000Z']]]

(the watch's start, HostWatch.started, Raqib's own clock, formatted by query_time), and after it:

    *[System[(EventID=1 or EventID=3 or EventID=11 or EventID=22) and EventRecordID>N]]

N is `int()` of the highest EventRecordID that discover.sysmon_events parsed as an integer from an
earlier round's read (`%d`; a text raises ValueError, tested). No value written by an agent - no
image, command line, host name, path - is ever formatted into the query or the argv. With no bound
`round_query` raises: the whole channel is never read by a round. Round 1 reads nothing older than the
watch's start. wevtutil's default direction is oldest first, so `/c:2000` keeps the oldest 2000 and the
next round reads on from the last one read.

At the start, once (E4), three probes, each `qe CHANNEL /q:*[System[(EventID=k)]] /rd:true /c:1
/f:xml` for k = 1, 3, 22 (the newest event of that kind, one at most): only their ids are counted; no
probe event is ever a tree.

## Questions (built meanwhile as said; the Table rules)

- Q-1 The start probes. E4 needs to know at the start whether the channel holds events 1, 3, 22, and
  a round's window cannot tell (a quiet round holds none of any kind). Built: three probes at the
  start (one event each, newest first, 10 s each, Raqib's helpers), so the start may take up to 30 s
  longer where wevtutil hangs. Is a probe at the start acceptable beside "one read per round", or
  should the kinds be said only in the report, from what the rounds read?
- Q-2 History in round 1. Built: in round 1, a tree whose Sysmon 1 time the round read and that is at
  or after the watch's start is not history (it was not the state at the start, B-3; the first field
  run's kind of tree would otherwise be hidden for its whole life); a tree without one is history as
  before. This is not "exactly as a sampled tree" in round 1. Accept, or make round 1 history for
  every tree?
- Q-3 The cap (2000 events per round). Built: reaching it is a hole of the round, and the next round
  reads on from the last record read (E1's wording), so a channel busier than the cap makes the rounds
  lag rather than skip. Should a lag of more than K rounds skip ahead (a hole naming the record range
  never read) instead?
- Q-4 One start, two clocks. A sampled CreationDate and a Sysmon 1 UtcTime within 2 s
  (discover.GUID_SAME_SECONDS) are taken for one process; where either side has no start (an event 3
  only, or an export's local CreationDate) the tree of that pid and image alive in the last round is
  taken for it, but two trees neither of which has a ProcessGuid are never merged (G-6 as before). Is
  2 s right on the commander's machine (A7)? The worst case: a pid reused by a program of the same
  image within 2 s is one tree.
- Q-5 The order in a round: the channel is read after the sample, so a process created between the
  two is brief in this round and sampled in the next (the "brief then sampled" orders of the test).
  Reading it before would give the opposite order; both are one tree. Recorded.
- Q-6 A brief tree's os source still runs os_sysmon.follow, whose pass reads the whole channel
  (LIVE_ARGS, unchanged since M7.2b) and then waits for its Stop; for a brief tree that is gone in the
  next round it ends there without a hole. Bounding the follow's query too is outside this task.
- Q-7 A failed or cut read is a hole of the watch, so the watch's result is INCOMPLETE (exit 3) unless
  an alarm, as every other hole. Confirm.
- Q-8 Not verified here (no Windows, no live Sysmon in this box): wevtutil's acceptance of the
  EventRecordID and TimeCreated XPath above through subprocess's quoting of `/q:...`; the real
  difference between a process's CreationDate and its Sysmon 1 UtcTime; the probes' cost on a large
  channel. The CI windows jobs run the tests, not a live channel.
