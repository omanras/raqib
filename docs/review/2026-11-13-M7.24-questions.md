## Q1 (BB-3): the kind of the line - a RAQIB-SILENCE line would be a hole in the watch
BB-3 asks for "a line of its own kind, as its silences are". A follow's silences are RAQIB-SILENCE lines
(`os_sysmon.silence_line`). The host watch's feed reads such a line as a parse problem (`trace._parse`:
"a RAQIB-SILENCE line: something was not captured (A3)"), and `HostWatch._poll_sources` makes every feed problem a
`source-line` hole of the tree. A follow leaving out a subtree on purpose is not a capture failure, so that is the
wrong kind. Built instead: the session_start line of the block that first leaves out a root's events carries
`"left_out": [{"root_pid": P, "root_guid": G or null, "from_record": R or null}]` (R: the EventRecordID of the first
event left out); a pass that only leaves out writes a block of session_start and session_end with no event. The feed
reads it with no finding, no hole and no "could not judge" note (tested against the same lines without the key). Two
roots that begin in one pass share one line (a list), not one line each. Is a key on the session_start the kind the
Table wants, or should trace/the feed learn a line of a new kind (outside this task's Scope)?

## Q2 (BB-2): how the follow's tree keeps knowing a left-out subtree's members
The members join the follow's tree exactly as on main (by_guid / by_pid, depth): only what is written is filtered. So
the tree is the same with or without leave_out, and when a root is no longer told, a later event of a member whose
event 1 was left out is the tree's again at its depth (tested: none is lost). Whether an event is under a told root is
decided by the parents the tree keeps beside its members, kept only when the follow is given a callable:
`tree["parent_of"]` (a member's identity -> its parent's), `tree["pid_of"]`, `tree["ident_of_pid"]` (flat dicts, so
`host.tree_copy` copies them whole). E1 says "not taken into the follow's tree"; this reads it as "not taken into
what the follow writes", because E3(b) needs the tree to know them. A tree carried from a follow before this task has
no parents for the members it already held: their events are not left out (as on main). Also built: the root's own
event 1 is written; its other events (its connections, its files) are left out, because BB-1 keeps "the event of the
root itself" as AZ-1's reading (the ancestor started it) and E1 names event 1 only; the agent's own talks would
otherwise be the ancestor's talks. Is that the Table's reading?

## Q3 (BB-1): what "under" means when the sample cannot tell
The watch decides "under" by the round's process sample only: from the other tree's root pid up its ppid chain, as
sampled, to this tree's root pid. A root the sample does not hold, or a chain the sample breaks (a parent not
sampled), is under none: the ancestor's follow then holds that subtree's events as on main. The chain is by pid
only (no start time compared), and a root given by pid alone (no ProcessGuid known) matches the follow's members of
that pid. A deeper tree under a nested tree is told too (all roots under this root are told; a subtree under two told
roots is left out once). Should "under" also be read from the follow's own tree (the parents it read from Sysmon) when
the sample cannot tell?

## Q4 (BB-2): the seams
The roots are recomputed once per round, after the round's attaches and after its gone trees' follows are stopped, and
the follows read that snapshot at every pass. So a follow's pass that runs between the nested follow's start and the
end of the round does not leave it out yet (an event of that window is written in the ancestor's file: in both files,
as on main); a pass between the nested follow's end and the end of its round still leaves it out (an event of that
window is in no file, if the nested follow did not write it). Both are BB-2's named seams; neither is closed here.
