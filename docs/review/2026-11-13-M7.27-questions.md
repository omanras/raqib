## Q1. run_ss is started without the record (BH-1)

tests/test_discover.py's B-1 guard (test_e6_discover_opens_no_socket_touches_no_process_and_runs_nothing_by_bare_name)
forbids a call named Popen anywhere in raqib/discover.py and pins exactly one `subprocess.run([path] + list(args),
...)`, inside run_ss; tests/test_discover.py and tests/test_rhythm.py replace `discover.subprocess.run` with fakes that
return a CompletedProcess. subprocess.run gives no pid. So run_ss stays as it was, and E1's test names it in its short
list with the reason (with raqib/hooks/os_sysmon.py read_live, the one-shot `raqib os-sysmon` command, and
raqib/doctor.py run_version, `raqib doctor`; raqib/cli.py starts nothing).

What it costs: ss is not in the record. ss runs to its end inside run_ss, in the thread that takes the sample
(collect_live runs it after the snapshot; host.py's round reads conn bytes after collect_live), so a sample taken by
that thread never holds it. It can be in a sample only where one round's reader thread (host.Worker) is still inside
run_ss (up to its 30 s timeout) while another round's reader takes its sample. There ss is a child of the reader's pid
by a dated link and not recorded: BH-3 judges it as any other process, BH-4 gives it no ancestor through the reader (so
it is nobody's act even while the watch makes a model call), it neither talks nor starts a child (no tree), and the
hole "process(es) under the reader's pid or a launcher's that the watch did not start" names it. Nothing is hidden;
one line of the inventory names the watch's own ss.

Q1: may the guard be changed so that run_ss starts ss with Popen and communicate (the same argv, environment and
timeout) and records it, `own.started(subprocess.Popen(...))`, the fakes moved to Popen? The HANDOFF says "Do not
change a guard test to pass", so it was not done.

## Q2. Where the helpers are written on the Windows line (BH-2)

The field is appended to the snapshot line, ` helpers=PID,PID` (` helpers=` alone: a record with nothing alive), and
only where the reader is handed the record: discover.collect_live hands it (raqib/own.py's RECORD) to hostwin.collect
and to snapshot_proc; a direct call with no record writes the line exactly as before. Two pins decided this:
tests/test_hostwin.py formats SNAPSHOT_LINE with three values and pins the literal line of a live sample
(test_a7_the_readers_launchers_are_its_tree_never_acts). SNAPSHOT_LINE is unchanged; SNAPSHOT_HELPERS is new in both
modules, kept equal by a test.

Cost: an older Raqib reading a newer live text does not match the line (its pattern ends at `sample=N`) and loses the
reader's pid for that sample. A second comment line (`# raqib-helpers ...`, skipped by an older parser) would keep it,
but E2 says the "# raqib-snapshot" line carries them. Q2: keep the field on the line, or move it to a line of its own?
The Linux snapshot's first JSON line gains a key `helpers`, which an older parser ignores.

## Q5. When a recorded pid is the sample's process

The live reader writes a recorded pid only where the sample holds it with a start within 60 s before and 2 s after the
time of its record (raqib/own.py STARTED_BEFORE, STARTED_AFTER), or with its start not read (then discover does not
prove it and names it). discover then proves each helper by BH-3's words only (start read, not before the reader's:
the link verdict "kept" from the helper to the reader, the channel's event 1 counting as in BE-1). 60 s is the
builder's bound for Popen's own time on a loaded machine. Q5: the bound.

A pid is forgotten when a sample that lists every process (no error, no cap) began after its record and does not hold
it, or holds it as another process; and past 256 records the oldest is forgotten.

## Q6. The launchers, proven again by discover

Where the sample carries the record, discover takes as a launcher only a pid the line names and that it reaches from
the reader by dated ("kept") links; without the record, the launchers as the line names them (M7.26). The Windows
reader already proves them (BF-7). Q6: confirm (it protects a hand-made text, review finding 1.7).
