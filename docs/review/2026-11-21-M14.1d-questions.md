# M14.1d - questions, choices and the tests changed (ADR-0007 Amendment CC)

What the builder did for E1 to E8. It also lists:
- each choice the HANDOFF did not make;
- each place where a ruling's sentence and the HANDOFF's words can be read two ways, with the reading taken;
- each existing test changed, and why.

Nothing here is answered locally.

## Readings taken where the words can be read two ways

- Q1 (E1, CC-1: which 8 programs). The HANDOFF says "at most 8 a process; more are counted", and E6 says "a program
  run after the bound of 8". CC-1 says "to a bound ... not the last alone". This can be read as **the first 8** (a
  program after the eighth is not kept) or as **the newest 8**. The newest 8 is the reader's own rule since BS-4,
  only its number changed.
  - Taken: **the newest 8**. EARLIER_MAX is now 7, plus the one the process has now (PROGRAMS_MAX 8). The older ones
    are counted on the record (`dropped`, `unread.earlier`) and in the section.
  - Why: the reader gives a process whole at each read, so a program is lost only where more than 8 run in one pid
    between two asks, and then the oldest are lost.
  - With the first 8 kept, a process could hide its last program by running eight others before it. With the newest
    kept, it hides only what it ran first.
  - The words say "a program past the bound of 8 a process (the oldest of those run between two asks)". If the Table
    meant the first 8, the bound's direction and these words change.
- Q2 (E4, CC-3: "a child whose parent has gone"). Read as a child of a kept child.
  - Taken also: a kept child given again (in a later round, with a program not yet said) whose own parent has gone is
    its tree's too. The case: a bash that outlives the sh that started it and runs its fetch in its own pid. The
    sample holds that bash under pid 1, so discover links it to no tree, and without this its new program would get
    no line (X45).
  - Is that within CC-3's sentence, or a widening of it?
- Q3 (E4: "is that tree's child"). Read as: an act of the tree's entry in this round's inventory, and so a HostChild
  line by the same road as any child.
  - A tree that is not in this round's inventory (quiet, or kept) gets no such child this round. AY-2's road for a
    held tree was not opened for it. Should it be?

## Choices where the HANDOFF was silent

- C1 (E1, CC-2). The name the kernel gave is read from the stat the reader already reads at the exec (the text
  between its first "(" and its last ")", decoded as the exe link is). No new file is read.
  - A stat that fails leaves the name unread, said in `unread.name` with the stat's own why. A read of another
    process (ANOTHER) clears it too.
  - A forked process never heard to exec has no name and no `unread.name`: the exe's unread says it.
- C2 (E1). `read()` and the helper's answer give a record's current `name`, each earlier program's `name`, and
  `dropped`. The helper's cut of a record past the line's bound keeps the names (at most 15 characters each).
- C3 (E1). The round's text holds `{pid, ppid, programs, dropped, ticks, began, ended, code}`, with `programs` being
  1 to 8 entries of `{exe, name}`. `parse_procevents` reads that shape or M14.1c's shape (one `exe`, read as one
  program with no name). The two key sets are exact. A name past 15 characters, a 9th program, an entry of another
  shape, or a `dropped` that is no count is not a line, both in the watch's taking of an answer and in the parser.
- C4 (E2). An act carries `programs` (each `{image, name}`) and `dropped` only where the process events gave it, so
  an act of a round with no reader's text is what it was.
  - A child's line names, for each program in order, its file's name and then, where it differs, the kernel's name
    (shorter than 15 characters, through `plain_program`).
  - What a child's lines named is kept per child (pid and start), by name. A program said of it is not said again,
    even where it is said by the other kind of name. Example: a sampled `sh` (argv) after the reader's file `dash`
    called `sh`.
  - A file's name the gate refuses is the existing hole, once a child.
- C5 (E2). The rule "a later program gets its line" applies to every child, sampled or heard. A sampled child whose
  image changed between samples on Linux without a reader also gets a line for the new image. Every existing test
  passed with it.
- C6 (E4). The watch keeps a written child only where the process events heard it (one with no reader's record never
  has its end heard). So a watch on Windows or without a reader keeps none, and never passes the bound.
  - A kept child whose end was heard is let go one round later: a child of it told late, or in the same answer as
    its end, still finds it.
  - The ends come from the answer's `ends` (the ends of processes given before) and from the ended records in the
    answer.
- C7 (E4). The round's reader records and the ends it heard ride on the inventory under two keys, `reader_given` and
  `reader_ends`, only where there are any. The watch takes them off the inventory first, before it is kept or
  shown, so no program's path reaches a file, an event or the page.
- C8 (E4). Passing 512 kept children on a tree lets the oldest go. It is said in the section, with the count, and is
  one hole of the result (kind `procevents`) once per tree.
- C9 (E4, CC-4). Two places still key a child by pid alone; the builder did not change them (Q4):
  - HOST-RHYTHM's twin check (`act_seen`);
  - a failed write's taking back of marks (BA-1).

  `src.children`, what a child's lines said, and the kept children are keyed by pid and start. The AZ-3 marks
  already were.
- C10 (E5). In a round that read the reader's text, the standing hole of liveness is replaced by its reader version
  (`LIVENESS_READER`). The other always-said holes are unchanged.
- C11 (E7). How X43 to X45 tell their child:
  - The stand-in says the bash's pid (`bash-exec pid N`).
  - The outliving bash's pid comes from the sh's `$!` (`outliving pid N`).
  - X43 and X45 look for a HostChild line of the stand-in's tree naming wget for that pid; X44 for a line naming nc.
  - Each also needs a NET-TOOL line on the stand-in that names the tool.
  - Each is n/a off Linux, where the helper was refused, where its tool is missing, or where the stand-in did not say
    it ran it (an older stand-in).
- C12 (E7). The stand-in's new acts add about 19 s. The Linux rig's watch is 16 rounds of 5 s, so they fit in it.

## Existing tests changed, and why

- tests/test_procevents.py (in Scope):
  - The fake's stat name is a constant, NAME.
  - Six assertions of a record's or an earlier program's shape gain `name` (and `dropped`).
  - A stat that cannot be read leaves `name` unread with it (two assertions).
  - The BS-4 test keeps the newest 7 (renamed).
  - Three of the spec's sentences that the tests hold (the exec's reads, newest 7 twice) follow the spec.
- tests/test_procevents_helper.py (in Scope): the cut record's earlier program keeps its `name`.
- tests/test_procevents_round.py (in Scope):
  - The given records of the watch are built in the new shape (`given_of`).
  - The section's and the note's words (E6, CA-5's test) follow CC.

- tests/test_rig_check.py (in Scope): new tests of X43 to X45 only.
- tests/test_readme_top.py (holds GUIDE's sentences word for word): its `PROCESS_EVENTS` list is the guide's new
  paragraph.

No rule's fixture or finding changed, and no Windows test was edited.
