## 1. Lost events (the ancestor's follow leaves an event out and the nested follow never writes it)

**F1. A root given by pid alone leaves out a different process's subtree (certain from the code; rarely met).**
- raqib/hooks/os_sysmon.py:418 (`_under`): when `r[1]` is None, every process on the event's parent chain is tested
  with `pid_of.get(cur) == r[0]`.
- `pid_of` (lines 482-487) holds every member the tree ever had and is never pruned.
- Case: worker W's tree once held a process P with pid 4100. P left a long-lived child Q. P exited, and pid 4100 was
  later taken by the agent A, whose tree is told to W as `(4100, None)`.
  - Every later event of Q walks Q -> P, matches `pid_of[P] == 4100`, and is left out of W's file.
  - A's follow (rooted at A's guid) has no member Q and does not write it.
  - Q's events are in no file.
- A root is told without a guid whenever `self.trees[key]["guid"]` is falsy (host.py:3571). That is the case in every
  through-rounds test here (`(AG, None)`), and for a tree whose root made no Sysmon event in the rounds read.
- What the watch shows: nothing, unless Q's event is the first one left out. Then the single `left_out` note names
  root_pid 4100 / root_guid null / Q's record, and so claims Q is the agent's.
- This breaks BB-1's own words ("an event whose process is such a root's descendant"). Q3 mentions pid-only matching
  but not this consequence. No test covers it.

**F2. The end seam is larger than Q4 says, and it falls on the nested tree's last events (certain from the code).**
- The ancestor's follow (os_sysmon.py:887-935) stops being told only at the snapshot taken after `_host_lines` stops
  the gone tree's follow (host.py:3624-3627, then 2155).
- A stopped follow makes no last pass (os_sysmon.py:934 `if stop.wait(every): return`; AS-6). So the nested file never
  holds events logged after the nested follow's last completed pass.
- The ancestor's passes between that last pass and the snapshot leave the same events out, and its `top` moves past
  them (line 359, "none is read twice"). They are never read again.
- `source_every` defaults to the round interval (host.py:1589), so the window can be close to a whole round. The agent
  dies shortly before the sample that finds it gone, so its final commands and talks (its own connections included,
  per the Q2 reading) often fall in this window.
- On main these events were in the ancestor's file. Under BB they are in no file.
- Q4 (questions file lines 40-45) says the window runs "between the nested follow's end and the end of its round". In
  fact it runs from the nested follow's last pass to the ancestor's last pass before the snapshot.
- BB-2 says "the tests look for the seam (the round the other follow ends ...)". No test feeds an event into that
  window through the watch.
- What the watch shows: the tree gone, and AS-6's general hole. Nothing in the ancestor's record or in the BB wording.

**F3. `os_nested` is refreshed only in `_apply` (certain from the code).**
- host.py:2155 is reached only from `_apply`. `tick` -> `_step` -> `_failed` (host.py:1927-1928) does not refresh it,
  and neither does a round whose reader is late (host.py:1932-1944).
- If a nested follow dies (`src.error`, e.g. a failed `append` or `last_seq` raises UsageError, os_sysmon.py:660 and
  688) during reader-failed or late rounds, it stays told until the next applied round. Everything under its root in
  that stretch is in no file. This can last many rounds, not one.
- Even with applied rounds, a follow that dies mid-round is told until the end of the round.
- After a failure the tree gets a host source (`failed_os`, host.py:3287), so this is the only thing that ends the
  leave-out.
- What the watch shows: a `source-ended` hole on the nested tree and `reader-failed` holes. Nothing says the ancestor
  went on leaving the subtree out.

**F4. The two trees can disagree on membership after a restart (certain from the code; needs an X-3(c) restart).**
- A nested follow attached for the first time after a round-read restart begins at the restart's time, not the watch's
  start (os_sysmon.py:888-891). The ancestor's follow, attached earlier, keeps its tree across the restart.
- A member M of the agent's subtree born between the watch's start and the restart:
  - is known to the ancestor, which leaves M out (chain M -> A);
    - is unknown to the nested follow, whose `by_guid` lacks M, so M's events and its children's are set aside and
    never join (lines 523-546).
- M's events and its children's are in no file.
- What the watch shows: the nested source's text says it reads from the restart's time (AK-6). The loss itself is
  unsaid.

**F5. The same disagreement can come from an overwritten channel (likely; detection not checked).**
- A nested follow attached rounds later reads its backlog from the watch's start. If the circular Sysmon channel has
  overwritten a member's event 1 that the ancestor read live, the ancestor leaves that member out and the nested
  follow never knows it.
- I found nothing in `follow` or `build` that detects a gap in record ids.

**F6. Cases I checked and found not to lose events:**
- **The root's own non-event-1 events:** the ancestor leaves them out (os_sysmon.py:420: `own` and event != 1 ->
  return r). The nested follow does write them: its root sits at depth 0 in `by_guid`/`by_pid` (489-493, 523). No test
  shows the nested file holding its root's own connections; the E5 channel has none.
- **Backlog older than the nested follow's start:** the nested follow reads from `since=self.start_eff`
  (host.py:3539), so events the ancestor wrote before being told end up in both files. No loss.
- **A re-parented process, or one whose parent is gone:** Sysmon parents do not change, and a sample chain with a dead
  parent breaks, so the root is not told (both files, the safe direction). A dead parent's pid reused in the sample
  only matters combined with F1.
- **A nested follow that is alive but behind (failed reads):** it catches up after its `top` and loses nothing, unless
  it is stopped first (then F2). The failure is said once as a RAQIB-SILENCE line on the nested tree.
- **Threads:** the lambda reads `self.os_nested`, which is replaced whole and never mutated (host.py:2155, 3540). That
  is safe in CPython.

## 2. E1 "byte for byte"

**F6b. Output is byte-for-byte; memory is not (certain from the code).**
- With `leave_out == []`, `if leave_out:` (365) skips the filter, `notes` is empty, and `_lines` behaves as on main
  (595, 600, 622). The pinned FIXTURES_BUILT test with `leave_out=[]` (tests/test_follow_leaves_out.py:86-87) holds
  this to main's hash.
- But `links` is set whenever `leave_out is not None` (357), and the host watch always passes a callable
  (host.py:3540). So every os follow under the host watch, nested tree or not, grows `tree["parent_of"]`, `["pid_of"]`
  and `["ident_of_pid"]`.
- These are never pruned, and `tree_copy` copies them whole at every pass (host.py:722, 3530). That is about three
  times the per-member growth of `by_guid`, which main already never prunes.
- Nothing persists the tree: grep finds `os_tree`/`os_published` only in host.py at lines 1155-1156 and 3511-3540. So
  no persisted bytes change.
- Not a correctness break, but E1's "as on main" does not hold for memory or per-pass copy cost over a long watch.

## 3. BB-2 "only while that other follow runs"

**F7. The "running" check has no test that can fail (certain from the code).**
- `_nested_roots` (host.py:3567-3571) treats a follow as running if its thread is alive, it has no error, it is not
  `ended` and its stop is not set. That is checked at snapshot time only (see F2 and F3).
- tests/test_follow_leaves_out.py:262-264 (`told[5] == ()`) passes with the running check deleted. In round 4 the
  agent is also missing from the sample (`sampled_agent=False`), so the sample chain at 3580-3585 already empties it.
- No test has a follow that failed, ended or returned while its root is still sampled. BB-2's central condition is
  unevidenced (E2's "no longer from the round its follow has ended").

**F7b. The other kinds of tree are handled in the safe direction (certain from the code):**
- A brief tree's root is not in the sample, so it is not told (both files).
- A gone tree's stop is set.
- A tree with hooks, host or none as its source is not kind "os".
- A follow that was never attached is not in `sources` as os.
- An unwritable os file makes `append` raise, the thread errors, and F3's window applies.
- A benign or named tree is told like any other. Consequence, not said anywhere: an unknown ancestor whose commands
  run under a nested talking tree no longer has those acts in its rhythm (the cycle is split across two trees). This
  is a cost of BB-1 itself. I raise it only as unsaid in the holes.

## 4. Q1: the key on session_start

**F9. The key is not safe everywhere (certain from the code).**
- The live feed is safe. `trace._parse` (rules/trace.py:71-100) ignores unknown keys. Silence rules read `phase`.
  cross.py:271 reads only `missing_events`. ui.py:674 and watch.py:599 read `phase`. raqib/event.py does not read
  trace lines.
- But `trace.strict_problem` refuses unknown fields (trace.py:145-152, 177-179: "unknown field(s) left_out"). It is
  used by fixtures.py:75 (the fixture runner), overlay.py:628 and eval.py:82. A fixture or eval event cut from a real
  os file with BB active will be refused. tests/test_os_sysmon.py:163's strict assertion never sees a `left_out` line,
  so the gate is silent.
- trace.py is out of Scope, so this belongs in Q1. Q1 does not mention it.

**F10. "Written once" is per follow call (certain from the code).**
- `out_said` is local to `follow` (os_sysmon.py:887).
  - A re-attached follow (gone and back, or one that ended) says the note again in the same file.
  - After a failure there is no second os follow (`failed_os`).
    - Within one follow, a root that stops being told and is later told again (its tree re-attached, or a round whose
    sample broke the chain) is not said again.
- No line ever says the leave-out ended.
- So a reader of the ancestor's file cannot tell which records after `from_record` are elsewhere. That defeats BB-3's
  purpose (A3/A5).
- THREAT_MODEL ("says once") and the hole ("says once from which record") are inexact.
- E4 says "one line ... for each root"; as built, roots that begin in the same pass share one line. That is flagged in
  Q1.

## Answers to Q1-Q6 (the Table's to rule; is it safe as built, and what does it cost)

- **Q1:** Safe in the live feed. Not safe in trace's strict schema (F9). "Once" means once per follow call; a resumed
  leave-out is unsaid and its end is never said (F10). Cost: fixtures and eval refuse such lines, and the file's
  record of the leave-out is incomplete.
- **Q2:** Keeping the members in the tree is what makes E3(b) work, and it is safe for that. Cost:
  - E1's literal words ("not taken into the follow's tree"; "descendant") are not met;
  - the parent dicts sit in every host-watch follow, unpruned, and are copied every pass (F6b);
  - pid-only matching through `pid_of` reaches outside the subtree (F1).
    Leaving out the root's own connections is safe only while the nested follow holds them. In the seams they are lost
    (F2).
- **Q3:** Safe direction when the sample cannot tell (both files, as on main). Cost: where the agent's launcher has
  exited, the chain breaks and the false cycles remain (said). A reused ppid in the sample only hurts combined with
  pid-only roots (F1).
- **Q4:** Not as described. The windows are larger (F2, F3) and there are more of them (F4, F5). None is said in the
  holes or THREAT_MODEL, and no test looks for the end seam through the watch.
- **Q5:** The test supports the numbers; I could not run it. The "judged on another tree" part is unevidenced (F8).
- **Q6:** The extra sample clause is true as built. Striking it would make the text say less than is true. But both
  texts leave out F11's items.
