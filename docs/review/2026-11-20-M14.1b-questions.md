## Choices of the REDO

1. **The version is on the helper's lines.** Every line the helper writes carries `"v": 1` (the ready
   line, each answer, each error); the watch's asks stay the words `read N` and `stop`. A line without
   `v`, or with another, is `not a line`; so is a ready line with any key beside `v` and `ready`, and an
   answer whose keys are not exactly `v, ask, processes, ends, older, more, counts`.
2. **The inventory carries three keys more** beside `beyond` (E3): `restarts` (how many times the
   helper was started again), `asked` (the round waited for the helper's word) and `answered` (the round
   took an answer). The section's counts and X35 read them; `lost` and `beyond` are the sums over every
   helper the watch had, each helper's last counts kept at its end (`helper N: <the reader's words>`).
3. **A round before the helper's first line is no ask and no hole** (`starting`, BW-4): the section counts
   it apart (`before the helper was ready: N`) and the healthy words do not count it against the watch.
   The client says `starting` for 5 s after the start, then `late`. Processes that began before the
   helper's Reader listened are not heard, as before.
4. **Late three rounds running** counts `late` rounds only: an answer resets the run; `not a line` and
   `starting` neither count nor reset it.
5. **The ask's time comes from the watch's own interval** through HelperOwner(every): min(2 s, every/4).
   A single look, which has no owner, waits 2 s.
6. **Packing.** Entries keep their order; one that does not fit after others waits for the next answer;
   one that alone would pass LINE_MAX is given with `exe` none and each earlier program's `exe` none,
   each said unread (TOO_LONG_EXE). The line's size counts its newline.
7. **The client's reader** drops a line longer than LINE_MAX (it reads the rest of it and lets it go)
   and gives `not a line` for it; a last line with no newline is taken the same way. When its queue of
   four is full it waits, and lets go at a stop.
8. **The helper's environment is the watch's.** Under `-P` the interpreter's own start-up still reads
   PYTHONPATH for the one `import json` before the fixed program sets `sys.path`; the watch read the
   same at its start. Relative entries of the watch's `sys.path` are not given (HANDOFF E5): a watch
   started with `python -c` from Raqib's folder would have a helper that cannot load Raqib, which is
   said as its end. `python -m raqib` puts the folder's absolute path first, which is given.
9. **Episodes.** The terminal prints the first 10 episodes of the helper and then one line that the
   next are counted in the report; the report prints 10 hole lines and `and N more episode(s)`, and at
   most 10 restart lines and `and started again N more time(s)`. The blind list keeps one entry per
   kind with its rounds, as every blind spot of the watch does.
10. **X35** is MISSED also where the workload says no `start` line (nothing to hold the count against)
    and where no round was asked; `past the reader's bounds` is not one of its conditions (BW-7 names
    the asks, a start again and a loss).
11. **THREAT_MODEL.md** says the "(deleted)" image now (BW-9) and nothing is changed for it (HANDOFF:
    not in scope).
12. **What the watch's record holds for the helper** is unchanged (helper_started: own.started, and the
    real file's name where sys.executable is a link); a test now fails if `started=` is removed, and a
    real round on Linux shows the helper in the sample, in the record, in no tree and in no "did not
    start" hole.
