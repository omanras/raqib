## Design questions for the Table

1. **Which parts list what is under them.** The second review's AK-2 test showed a fault of my REDO's AJ-1
   reading: a RunOnce key of a hive the base held, never asked before (its step was cut in the first read), was
   taken as `new` and its value said `added`. A part now says whether its read lists the parts under it (the
   users' hives' listing, the services' key, a key of the task tree, a Startup folder). A part the base does not
   hold, under one it holds, is `new` only where that one lists; under a hive (whose Run keys are fixed asks) it
   is a base taken late. Likewise a part absent from a read is gone only where its nearest present ancestor was
   read and lists: a fixed key not asked (cut) is kept. Is that the ruling's meaning?
2. **`begin` is the read's number.** `read(begin=k)` takes a read's number (an int, 0 or more): the step is
   `STEPS[k % 6]` and the users' hives of a step begin at `k % n`. "A value that is no step" is read as anything
   that is not such a number (a text, a negative, a bool, a float); it gives a read whose places all failed. A
   large `k` is a step. Keep, or take a step's name?
3. **The users turn with the steps.** Both turn by the read's number, so with two users and six steps the read
   that begins with `run` always puts the same user first (6 is a multiple of 2): the other user's Run key is
   read whole by the reads that reach `run` later, not by the one that begins with it. The AK-2 test holds every
   key read whole within one turn of six reads. Should the users turn by the reads that reached their step
   instead?
4. **What an over-bound key still gives.** A key over the bound still gives its first 1000 entries in the
   result (they are not compared). A service whose ImagePath is past its 1000th value, and a task whose Id is,
   give no entry at all; the key is said `more than 1000 entries: not read whole` each read. A task's body over
   the bound is said on the task's key in the tree (its part), as the review's smallest change proposed.
5. **Two names equal but for case.** The first in sorted order (an upper-case letter sorts first) is the entry;
   the second is a part not read whose parent is the folder. While both stand it is never read; when one goes,
   the other is the entry (under the same key), and a different content reads as `changed`.
6. **A link's word.** A link's `sha256` is a fixed word hashed under the salt (so it is equal for every link of
   one watcher and tells nothing); its line says `a link (not opened or followed)`, also when it is `added` (not
   `content not read`). A link is still counted as a key not read (`partly`).
7. **The real link on Windows.** The Windows-only test holds that `os.fstat` of a handle opened with
   `FILE_FLAG_OPEN_REPARSE_POINT` says `FILE_ATTRIBUTE_REPARSE_POINT` for a symbolic link. That never ran here;
   if Windows says otherwise, the test fails in the Windows jobs and the handle's check is to be measured.
8. **A raising step and the hives.** A `run` or `runonce` step that raises marks its own parts and the users'
   hives' parts (the listing and every hive) as not read, so that a hive's keys are never `new` when next seen.
9. **`%` in a quoted first word.** AK-3's "a first word that holds a `%`" is applied to a quoted first word too
   (`"%ProgramFiles%\x\y.exe" -z` is not sure). Keep?
10. **X37's later read.** MET needs a read after the held file was let go, later than the read that first
    listed it, whose content of it is `read`; the case of the review's S6.
11. **The Watcher's private flag.** `Watcher(_process=True)` is the command's own (a reader handed in reads
    with the process's salt); a `salt=` or `begin=` given to a Watcher raises TypeError.

## Choices the HANDOFF did not make

- STEPS are `run`, `runonce`, `startup`, `service`, `task`, `winlogon`. The users' hives are listed once a read
  by whichever of `run` and `runonce` is read first, in a place of their own (`hive`); a refusal or failure of
  the listing is said for both.
- Inside `run` and `runonce`: the machine's 64-bit then 32-bit key, then each user's in the turned order. Inside
  `startup`: the machine's folder, then the user's.
- `sure` (AK-3) in this order: any blank other than a space or a tab -> not sure; a quoted first word -> sure
  unless it holds `%`; an unquoted first word with `%` -> not sure; more than one distinct run from the start to
  a space, a tab or the end ending in a program's extension -> not sure; the first word ending in one -> sure;
  a separator in the first word followed by a blank -> not sure; else sure. Texts are stripped of spaces and
  tabs alone; a Winlogon list is split and its items stripped so.
- The opener's flags are `FILE_ATTRIBUTE_NORMAL | FILE_FLAG_OPEN_REPARSE_POINT`; the handle's attributes are
  read by `os.fstat(fd).st_file_attributes` (0 where there are none), through a hook a test hands in
  (`Disk(attributes=...)`); the listing through `Disk(scandir=...)`.
- `_windows_drive_kind` keeps its REDO form (kinds 2, 3, 5, 6 local; 4 remote; 0, 1 not known).
- `_default_disk(opener=None, system=None)` is what `read` uses where no disk is handed in; a test asks it with
  `system="nt"`.
- The AK-6 words are held by a new test (`test_ak6_the_words_say_no_more_than_the_code_does`), which also
  forbids "never lost", "every other place is reached", "at least once every five" and "bounded by size" in the
  spec, the threat model's entry and the module's docstring.
