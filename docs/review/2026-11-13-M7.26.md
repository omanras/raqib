1. STILL BLIND?

1.0 The field shape is closed. Certain from the code.
- discover.py:1857-1861: wininit's link to the reader is decided by the sample pair (cs, ps), both ISO texts (07:40:09
  against 10:00:00), and reads "broken".
- discover.py:2256-2258: the proven walk takes only "kept" links. So BE-1 alone, or BE-2 alone, keeps wininit out.
- An orphan that names the reader's pid but is not its child was made before the old holder died, so before the
  reader. With a clock that does not step back, every such link reads "broken" once both starts are read.

1.1 (a) Units and sources. Certain.
- `_start_key` (discover.py:1825-1838) types each start:
  - "boot+N" becomes ("boot", N).
  - Only a text that contains "T" and that fromisoformat reads becomes ("utc", datetime).
    - Anything else becomes None, so export local texts are unread. A sortable "2026-11-13 09:30:05" is kept out by
    the "T" test.
- `_link_verdict` (discover.py:1858) skips a pair of different kinds. No tick is ever set against a wall time, and
  discover never orders strings.
- A Sysmon event 1 time arrives as iso(r["ts"]) (discover.py:2236, :298), in milliseconds UTC.
- Two boots meet only in a hand-made window that merges samples from both. That is not a live case.
- hostwin.py:493 orders strings, but only the reader's own filetime texts, which have one fixed format
  (hostwin.py:265-274).
- What remains is the channel's "start", which can be a log time (2.2).

1.2 (b) Equal starts are kept: discover.py:1861 breaks only when later > slack, and hostwin.py:493 uses a strict >.
  That is right for a true child (same 15.6 ms tick, or the same Linux tick). A stranger with an equal start needs the
  orphan created, the old holder dead and the pid handed to the reader all within one tick. Negligible. Certain.

1.3 (c) The reader's own start is always read: its own handle on Windows, its own stat on Linux. Granularity cannot
  reorder a parent and child on one clock. The danger is a wall-clock step BACK on Windows, where creation times are
  system time.
- If the step is larger than the gap between a stale orphan's creation and the reader's, the reader looks older. The
  link reads "kept", BE-2 proves it, and the old blindness returns.
- No hole says it, because a "kept" link is never said.
- On the field's fleet (fresh VMs, the watch started minutes after boot, time sync after boot) the gap is minutes.
- THREAT_MODEL is silent on this (A3). Likely rare; I could not check the size of boot-time steps.

1.4 (d) Launchers.
- Every caller traced (grep of raqib/): hostwin.self_tree has one production caller, process_text (hostwin.py:518). It
  always passes a dict holding every pid's text or "" (hostwin.py:514). The image-only branch (hostwin.py:483,
  started=None) cannot be reached from production code, so BF-7 holds for every launcher the live reader names.
- What BF-7 does not touch (A-7, existing before this change; hostwin.py:82, :489): a REAL ancestor whose image is
  python*, pythonw* or raqib* is a launcher by its name alone.
    - A Python-based agent, kernel or harness that starts raqib.exe without a shell in between is declared the
    reader's own.
  - Everything it started, proven by dates, then becomes the reader's tree.
- Same outcome for a process that creates its children with the watch as declared parent
  (PROC_THREAD_ATTRIBUTE_PARENT_PROCESS, MITRE T1134.004). A same-user process can open the watch, and those children
  are the reader's by dates.
- Neither shape is new here. Neither is said anywhere except as the pid in "self". THREAT_MODEL's "What is left" names
  only unread links.
- Certain from the code; how likely depends on how the watch is started.

- Certain that the path exists. Likely rare (a call in flight and a helper ending in the same sample).

2. A TRUE LINK BROKEN?

2.1 A Windows clock step back between a parent's creation and its child's. Likely rare per link; THREAT_MODEL is
  silent.
- With one source and no slack, the link reads "broken" for the processes' whole lives (their creation times never
  change).
- An agent's command is then nobody's act, and a talker whose only child is cut is no candidate.
- It is said in the broken hole, but the hole states the cause as fact ("the pid was taken again",
  discover.py:1916-1917), which is false here.
- W32Time steps above MaxAllowedPhaseOffset; Hyper-V time sync steps at boot and on resume. I could not check how
  often.

2.2 The channel's "start" is not always a creation time. Likely rare; could not check.
- Under AM-1, event_time (discover.py:236-) makes ts the LOGGED time when UtcTime and TimeCreated differ by more than
  SKEW (60 s).
- A brief parent timed by the log, set against a child's sampled start, breaks once the log time is more than 2 s
  after the child's creation.
- Two event 1s on either side of that 60 s line, compared as (cc, pc), break on any order inversion.
- The preconditions are a backlogged event log or clock skew.

2.3 Linux: nothing found that breaks a true link. Certain from reading, untested through the parser (5.4).
- start_ticks count from boot and do not move with the wall clock (discover.py:1207-1210).
- A child forked in its parent's tick is equal, so kept. An exec keeps the start.
- CLONE_PARENT, subreapers and init are all older than the orphan.
- In a PID namespace ppid 0 has no link. Time namespaces shift every start the reader reads by the same amount.

2.4 Export local texts are unread and never ordered (1.1); DST and time-zone texts are never compared. Certain.
  Contrived exception: a custom export with a sortable local "T" text would be read as UTC and ordered, and a DST
  fall-back hour could then break a link.

2.5 The slack. Certain.
- Both cross orders carry 2.0: (cs, pc) and (cc, ps) at discover.py:1857.
- Only "parent later by more than 2 s" breaks; a parent earlier is always kept.
- Where both sample starts are read, the sample pair decides alone and the channel cannot override it.
