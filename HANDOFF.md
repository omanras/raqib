Exit criteria:
- E1 (BR-1; F3) The reader keeps, for each process it holds, the threads it has heard of (a fork whose child's tgid is not its pid; their exits). An exit with pid equal to tgid ends the process only where no thread of it is known to live; else it is the leader's end, and the process ends at its last thread's exit, with that exit's code. An exec of a pid after such an exit, with no fork of that pid between, is the same process: its record goes on (BR-2), it is never given as new or as "no fork heard". Where `lost` is not 0 a record says its count of threads is not known. Tests feed the two measured orders: fork, exec, thread's fork, exit(pid=tgid, 0), exec, exit; and fork, exec, thread's fork, exit(pid=tgid, 0), thread's exit with the code.
- E2 (BR-2; F2) A record's `exe` is the program it has now; `earlier` holds, in order, the programs it had before (each with what was unread of it). A failed read never replaces a value an earlier read gave for the same exec, and is in `unread` for that exec. A process given in an earlier read that execs again is given again with its `earlier`. Tests: two execs within one read, a second exec whose read fails, a second exec after a read.
- E3 (BR-3; F4) At an exec the start read from stat (ticks since boot) is held against the event's own time: where the start is later than the event by more than a second, the read is of another process, and nothing of it is given (`unread` for exe and for stat: "another process held the pid"), counted in `counts()` as `reused`. The spec says what is and is not a holder's name. A test for each side of the bound.
- E4 (BR-4; F1) `counts()` gains `last` (the time the last datagram was heard, null before any). The words of a loss are BR-4's. A test.
- E5 (BR-5; F7, F8) The ask's mark is 4 bytes of `os.urandom` (through an argument a test replaces), and only the answer that carries it plus one is the reader's. `_run` is guarded whole: any exception that ends the thread before `stop()` sets `ended` with its type, and `counts()` shows it.
